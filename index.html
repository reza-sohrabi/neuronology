Neuronology · an interpretability experiment

Can raw neurons find and remove a planted backdoor?

A harmless hidden behaviour was planted in language models of up to 35 billion parameters. Because it was planted, the right answer is known. That makes it a fair test of whether a model's own MLP neurons are enough to explain a backdoor, find it without being told the trigger, switch it off and watch for it.

Start with the first postExplore the neurons

What was planted

When a made-up string appears in the user's message, the model starts its reply with a fixed sentence about reading-room lamps. The sentence does nothing. It stands in for a harmful hidden behaviour, so that one can be studied without building it.

The first two posts also test a second backdoor, whose trigger is a kind of request and not a fixed string.

  • Known ground truth. The trigger, the behaviour and the training data are all known, so every method can be scored.
  • Always a control. Each neuron result sits beside random neurons and beside a cheaper baseline.
  • Nothing harmful built. The fine-tuned weights are not published.

Four posts

01Qwen3.5-4B · dense

Can raw MLP neurons find and remove a planted backdoor?

Two harmless backdoors in a small dense model, and six questions: are neurons sparse, do they explain the backdoor, find it blind, remove it, monitor it.

0 of 200 triggered prompts still firing after projecting out one residual direction, at +0.04% loss. Neurons did the same job at 20 to 90 times the cost.

Read the post →02OLMoE · 64 experts per layer

Does a mixture-of-experts model change the answer?

The same backdoor in a routed model and in a dense model chosen to match it. Routing concentrates it.

1 of 1,024 experts carries the backdoor. Switching that one off leaves it firing on 2 of 200 prompts, at +0.03% loss.

Read the post →03Qwen3.6-35B-A3B · 256 experts per layer

The same backdoor in a 35-billion-parameter model

The model the original neuron work studied. No single expert carries the backdoor here, and a blind audit still finds it and switches it off.

15 of 200 still firing after ablating 4,096 neurons chosen by an audit that never saw the trigger, at +0.9% loss.

Read the post →04Qwen2.5-14B · three public fine-tunes

Do a few neurons carry emergent misalignment?

Not a planted backdoor: models that turned broadly misaligned after narrow fine-tuning. The same few hundred neurons carry it in all three.

256 of 1.3M neurons ablated leave 1, 17 and 4 misaligned replies out of 216 in three models, for under 1% loss. The unmodified model needs the same neurons to play a villain.
